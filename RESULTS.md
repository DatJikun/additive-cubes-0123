## What we claim as NEW

After a second literature search (OEIS, abelian-crucial-word papers, Liétard, Rao, Andrade–Mol, GitHub), the following are **not** in those sources.

### NEW 1. Shortest right-crucial ACF words over {0,1,2,3} have length 14
**Class:** COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED theorem.  
**Type:** G — structural characterization of the finite language.

A word is *right-crucial* (a dead-end) if it is ACF but none of the four one-letter extensions is ACF.

- There are **0** right-crucial ACF words of length ≤ 13.
- There are **8170** right-crucial ACF words of length 14.
- They form **4083** orbits under the group generated by reversal and complement \(a\mapsto 3-a\).

**Witness** (independently checked by C++ prefix-sum verifier and Python triple-loop):
```
w = 00103010301100
```
`w` is ACF. The four extensions fail as follows:

| letter | cube (i, d, sum) | pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | i=12, d=1, sum=0 | ordinary cube `000` at the end |
| 1 | i=9, d=2, sum=1 | additive, not ordinary |
| 2 | i=0, d=5, sum=4 | additive, spans the whole word |
| 3 | i=3, d=4, sum=4 | additive |

Full list: `data/deadends14.txt`.  
Reproduce: `./bin/dump_deadends` and `python3 python/brute_verify.py` on any line.

**Novelty check.** Glen–Simpson–Smyth (DMTCS 2011) determine *abelian*-cube crucial lengths; over 4 letters that length is **20**. No source lists additive-cube crucial words over {0,1,2,3}. Liétard counted ACF words only to n=8 and did not discuss dead-ends. OEIS has ordinary cubefree counts (A051043), not additive.

**Why it matters.** Additive cubes are a weaker prohibition than abelian cubes, so one expects earlier dead-ends; 14 vs 20 is the first measured comparison. It also shows the ACF trie is *not* a complete 4-ary tree past depth 13, but the dead-end fraction at 14 is small relative to c₁₂=6.77×10⁶.

### NEW 3. Exhaustive failure of 2-uniform morphisms
**Class:** COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED certificate (sharpening of Liétard’s size≥7).

All 4⁸ = 65,536 two-uniform morphisms, iterated from all four seeds to length 250: **no ACF iterate of length 250**. Maximum ACF prefix length: **119**.

Liétard already concluded that a Cupisca morphism, if it exists, has size ≥7. This file is an explicit, reproducible classification of the uniform size-2 slice with a concrete worst-case morphism and a hard length bound.

Reproduce: `./bin/acf_tool uniform 2 250`

### NEW 3b. Exhaustive failure of primitive 3-uniform morphisms
**Class:** COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED certificate (Theorem AG). See `PT_REPORT.md`.

All \(4^{12}=16{,}777{,}216\) three-uniform maps; 6{,}116{,}013 primitive prolongable with \(\ge 3\) image sums. None has an ACF iterate of length 2187. Maximum ACF prefix: **1647**, map \(0\mapsto 100,\ 1\mapsto 102,\ 2\mapsto 132,\ 3\mapsto 332\), seed 1, cube \((982,222,229)\). Dual C++/Python. This is not a proof for \(m\ge 4\) and not an infinite ACF word.

### NEW 4. Explicit obstruction for the best size-3 Cassaigne-shaped morphism
**Class:** COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED counterexample.

Among 1,048,576 morphisms of shape (3,3,1,3), one prefix reached the search cap 300:
```
0 → 001,  1 → 031,  2 → 1,  3 → 233
```
Its fixed-point prefix is ACF up to length 300 and has an additive cube
`i=201, d=59, sum=66` ending at position 378. Neighbourhood search (all 1-symbol mutants, 2000 random 2-symbol mutants) did not improve 378.

This is the adversarial loop working: a survivor at cap 300 is not a construction.

### NEW 5. High-complexity diagnosis of Up-and-Down words
**Class:** COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED.

A 400,000-letter Up-and-Down word (period 2000) contains every ACF factor of length 6. It cannot be a prefix of a purely morphic infinite word of linear complexity. The 70M-type objects are not hidden substitutions.

## What we reconstructed, not new

- Length 24,396 with fixed order (exact match to Liétard).
- Up-and-Down mechanism; archived ACF words of lengths 65,986 and 400,000. **Record remains 70,880,000.**
- Cassaigne morphism prefix of 20,000 over {0,1,3,4}.

## What we did not achieve

- No infinite ACF word over {0,1,2,3}.
- No new finite record.
- No proof ruling out all morphisms of size ≥7.
- No progress on Green’s Problem 15 (additive squares / 3-APs).
- The universal unaligned letter-desubstitution theorem is false; see `DESUBSTITUTION_REPORT.md` (Theorems J, K, L).
- No force-cubes theorem for non-affine uniform morphisms. Krylov escape and CCSS-style length templates do not supply one (`TEMPLATE_REPORT.md`, Theorems V–Z). The remaining bottleneck is whether an *automatic* 4-letter numerical word can avoid \(E_\sigma\)-cubes.
- No finite-memory self-sustaining core: Theorems AI–AK. Isolated \(q=1\) from length 8; length-14 dead-ends are leaves with live siblings; Up-and-Down is not a nested recurrence (`CORE_REPORT.md`).

### NEW 6. No all-paths finite-state ACF core
**Class:** PROVED (elementary periodic points) plus computational kills of operators/templates. Not an infinite word.

## Status of the original problem

**Still open.** Finite-state geometry cannot certify global ACF. Unbounded-memory search and large morphisms remain.
